# Rendering papers worth adopting in radiance (survey, 2026-10-06)

## Top 12 by leverage

1. **Physically Based Shading at Disney (2012) and Extending the Disney BRDF to a BSDF (2015)** (Brent Burley, SIGGRAPH Course: Physically Based Shading in Theory and Practice, 2012)
   - Why: It is the biggest realism step: one GGX-based material record replaces the two toy materials (diffuse and mirror), and items 5, 6, 7, 9 and the grad goal build on it.
   - Milestone: M3. Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: medium.
   - Detail: Record 0004 (material record) and record 0001 (material words). Write Schlick's (1-cos)^5 as products, not pow, so that the lobe choice obeys record 0005 rule 2. MIS between NEE and BSDF-hit emission is a prerequisite: trace.shade.ts adds emission only after a specular bounce or on the camera ray.
   - https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/48/asset/s2012_pbs_disney_brdf_notes_v3.pdf

2. **BRDF Importance Sampling for Polygonal Lights** (Christoph Peters, ACM TOG 40(4), SIGGRAPH, 2021)
   - Why: Solid-angle sampling, then projected-solid-angle sampling, of the chosen emissive triangle removes the 1/r^2 noise of area sampling near the Cornell box's ceiling light. It is the cheapest visible noise cut in today's NEE.
   - Milestone: quality wave before M3. Depth: kernel (record 0005 amendment if an angle steers). Cost: small.
   - Detail: Only nee() in trace.shade.ts changes. No new buffer. The solid-angle formula uses atan and the sampled direction depends on it. Write it with turn() and products, or amend rule 2.
   - https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/polygonal_lights.php

3. **SZ Sequences: Binary-Constructed (0, 2^q)-Sequences** (Abdalla G. M. Ahmed et al., ACM TOG, SIGGRAPH Asia (arXiv 2505.20434), 2025)
   - Why: New generator matrices give 4D stratification to the dimensions of each bounce. Burley scrambling and the integer-only sampler stay. The paper reports up to 1.93x lower error.
   - Milestone: quality wave before M3. Depth: kernel. Cost: small.
   - Detail: Only sampler.shade.ts changes: SZ matrices replace the padded 2D Sobol. First confirm that a module-level const u32 array compiles on all three outputs. If it does not, open a compiler proposal.
   - https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/ahmed2025/

4. **Filter Importance Sampling** (Manfred Ernst et al., IEEE Symposium on Interactive Ray Tracing, 2006)
   - Why: The pixel jitter goes through the filter's inverse CDF, so a better filter than the implicit box costs no splatting and no atomics.
   - Milestone: quality wave before M3. Depth: kernel. Cost: small.
   - Detail: Only the jitter in trace.shade.ts changes. Use a tent filter (sqrt only), or a tabulated Blackman-Harris or Mitchell CDF in the uniform block. A Gaussian through Box-Muller needs log and cos to set the ray, which record 0005 rule 2 forbids.
   - https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Sampling_and_Reconstruction/Image_Reconstruction

5. **Sampling Visible GGX Normals with Spherical Caps** (Jonathan Dupuy et al., HPG 2023 (CGF 42(8)), 2023)
   - Why: It is the simplest and cheapest VNDF sampler for every GGX lobe of item 1, and it uses the standard VNDF pdf, so MIS needs no change.
   - Milestone: M3. Depth: kernel. Cost: small.
   - Detail: sampleBsdf in materials.shade.ts. The cap's azimuth goes through turn().
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05044

6. **Practical multiple scattering compensation for microfacet models (with Kulla and Conty 2017)** (Emmanuel Turquin, ILM technical report, 2019)
   - Why: A directional-albedo table and one multiply stop rough metals and rough glass from going dark. It also makes a white-furnace gate possible.
   - Milestone: M3. Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: small.
   - Detail: No storage slot is free: the pipeline binds 7 and slot 8 is reserved for the console (record 0001 rule 1). Put a 32x32 f32 table (4 KiB) in the uniform block or append it to the material buffer, and amend records 0001 and 0004. Interpolate in code, not with hardware filtering.
   - https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/turquin/ms_comp_final.pdf

7. **MIS Compensation: Optimizing Sampling Techniques in Multiple Importance Sampling (with env-map NEE)** (Ondrej Karlik et al., ACM TOG 38(6), SIGGRAPH Asia, 2019)
   - Why: HDRI NEE with MIS is the large gain under image-based lighting, and the compensation, done in host preprocessing, adds about 2.75x lower NMSE at no run-time cost.
   - Milestone: M3. Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: small.
   - Detail: Record 0001 (the CDFs need room in an existing buffer, since no slot is free) and the lights part of record 0004. A texture-based env map waits on the texture-array binding convention in plan section 9.
   - https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/~jaroslav/papers/2019-mis-compensation/

8. **Importance Sampling of Many Lights with Adaptive Tree Splitting** (Alejandro Conty Estevez et al., HPG 2018 (PACMCGIT 1(2)), 2018)
   - Why: A light BVH in place of the power table stops NEE from wasting samples on far lights and lights that face away. Item 13's SG importance and later ReSTIR candidates build on it.
   - Milestone: M3. Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: medium.
   - Detail: Record 0001: the tree replaces the light-table buffer, with no new slot. Record 0005: the orientation-cone importance decides the child, so compute it with cosine identities, not acos. Without splitting, the descent is a short loop.
   - https://doi.org/10.1145/3233305

9. **Optimised Path Space Regularisation (builds on Kaplanyan and Dachsbacher 2013)** (Philippe Weier et al., EGSR 2021 (CGF 40(4)), 2021)
   - Why: After NEE has met a diffuse vertex, it adds a small tunable roughness to the mirror and glass, so NEE can connect through them. This removes most fireflies and black regions on diffuse-specular-diffuse paths at almost no run-time cost.
   - Milestone: M3 (after the GGX lobe exists). Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: small.
   - Detail: Record 0004 (path-type state and a mollification table). Record 0002: the golden must state that the regularised mode is biased. The CPU oracle runs the same schedule. Kaplanyan's shrinking schedule comes first.
   - https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14347

10. **Firefly Removal in Monte Carlo Rendering with Adaptive Median of meaNs (G-MoN)** (Jerome Buisine et al., EGSR 2021 (Symposium Track), 2021)
   - Why: It removes the mirror and caustic fireflies before any denoiser sees them, with no atomics and a consistent estimate.
   - Milestone: quality wave before M3 (plan section 7 puts firefly handling at M1 and M4). Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: small.
   - Detail: Record 0001 (the accumulator becomes k buckets in its one buffer). Choose the bucket by frame index, not sample index. Then each frame still writes once (record 0005 rule 4), and the write stays outside the sample loop (record 0007 rule 3). The present pass computes the median and the Gini blend. M5 fitting reads the plain mean.
   - https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/sr20211296

11. **Efficient Incoherent Ray Traversal on GPUs Through Compressed Wide BVHs** (Henri Ylitie et al., HPG, 2017)
   - Why: It is the best software traversal without RT cores: about 2x on incoherent secondary rays, which buys about 2x spp at equal time.
   - Milestone: quality wave before M3 (performance). Depth: kernel plus record amendment. Cost: medium.
   - Detail: Record 0001 fixes the node layout from M2 through M3, so changing it needs an amendment. The host collapses the SAH BVH into 8-wide nodes. The BLAS loop uses countOneBits and firstLeadingBit (both in the surface) and bitcast from an array<vec4u>. Break ties in t by primitive index.
   - https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2017-07_efficient-incoherent-ray-traversal-gpus-through-compressed-wide-bvhs

12. **Screen-Space Blue-Noise Diffusion of Monte Carlo Sampling Error via Hierarchical Ordering of Pixels (ZSobol)** (Abdalla G. M. Ahmed et al., ACM TOG 39(6), SIGGRAPH Asia, 2020)
   - Why: A scrambled Morton index turns low-spp error into blue noise. The site preview looks cleaner, and a later denoiser removes the noise more easily.
   - Milestone: quality wave before M3. Depth: kernel. Cost: small.
   - Detail: Only the sample index in sampler.shade.ts changes. The blue-noise property needs a power-of-two spp budget known in advance. The site's caps (1,024 and 256) already are one.
   - https://doi.org/10.1145/3414685.3417881

## avoid

- Hardware RT dependence: DOBB-BVH and the 2026 wavefront note measured their gains with hardware ray tests. radiance traverses in software, so expect smaller or no gains, and measure first.
- Float atomics: WGSL has only atomic<u32> and atomic<i32>, and record 0005 rule 4 forbids atomics on the accumulation path. Splatting filters, path-guiding tree splats, Markov-chain cell updates, hash-grid radiance caches and neural training all assume float atomic adds. Use per-pixel slots and one-order reductions (rule 5) instead, or skip the method.
- Non-deterministic reductions and allocation: atomic-add node allocation (PLOC++, H-PLOC, wavefront queues) makes the buffers differ between runs. Use prefix-sum allocation and an index tie-break.
- Wavefront restructuring and buffer pressure: ReSTIR reuse, GRIS, Area ReSTIR and a wavefront split need passes and per-pixel buffers. The trace pipeline already binds 7 storage buffers and keeps the eighth for the console (record 0001 rule 1). Record 0007 rule 3 allows a WebGL2 megakernel entry at most 4 scalar or vector writes per path, none in a loop.
- Transcendentals that steer: record 0005 rule 2 forbids sin, cos, exp, log, pow, atan and acos in a comparison, an index or a lobe choice. Box-Muller Gaussian jitter, acos in light-tree cones, pow-based Fresnel lobe selection and atan in solid-angle sampling must be rewritten with turn(), products or cosine identities, or else rule 2 must be amended.
- Subgroups and f16: record 0005 rule 6 forbids them in gated kernels, and plan section 9 places both after 1.0. H-PLOC, neural guiding and NRC depend on them.
- Temporal reuse in the converging mode: ReSTIR temporal reuse, SVGF history and Lumen's caches correlate frames and are biased. Keep them out of the golden and differential gates, or gate them in a separate mode.

## All papers collected (by topic)

- [sampling] Practical Hash-based Owen Scrambling (Brent Burley (sole author), JCGT (Journal of Computer Graphics Techniques) vol. 9 no. 4 2020). Owen scrambling of a Sobol sequence becomes a few integer operations: reverse the bits, apply a Laine-Karras style hash keyed by a per-dimension seed, and reverse the bits again. A hashed shuffle of the sample index then pads any number of dimensions from only the first 4 Sobol dimensions. Fit: High. All integer u32 math: multiply, xor, shift and reverseBits. WGSL has these natively, so the result is bit-identical across vendors and matches the CPU oracle exactly. Convert to f32 with (x >> 8) * 2^-24 so the cast is exact. Padded Sobol needs only 4 x 32 direction numbers. These fit in a const array, so the sampler uses none of the 8 storage buffers. WebGL2: GLSL ES 3.00 has no bitfieldReverse, so the tier needs a 5-step shift-and-mask emulation. The emulation is still exact. Grad: the sampler gives the shading function fixed, low-variance inputs. Use a different seed for the adjoint pass if the gradient estimator needs decorrelation from the primal pass. Cost: small. https://jcgt.org/published/0009/04/01/
- [sampling] Screen-Space Blue-Noise Diffusion of Monte Carlo Sampling Error via Hierarchical Ordering of Pixels (Z-sampler, the basis of pbrt-v4 ZSobolSampler) (Abdalla G. M. Ahmed et al. (with Peter Wonka), ACM TOG 39(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2020). Order the pixels along a Morton (Z) curve and randomly permute the digits of the Morton index (scramble the Z order). Then give each pixel a consecutive block of one global Owen-scrambled Sobol sequence. Each pixel stays low-discrepancy, and the error between neighbouring pixels becomes blue noise in screen space. Fit: High. Only integer bit operations, no tables, no extra buffers. Deterministic and identical on the CPU oracle. Same WebGL2 note as Burley (emulate bit reversal). Limit: the blue-noise property needs the spp budget known in advance. Open-ended progressive accumulation past the budget must restart with a new seed, and it then loses the screen-space blue noise. Grad: neutral to positive. Fitting loss images at low spp get less clumped error. Cost: small. https://doi.org/10.1145/3414685.3417881
- [sampling] A Low-Discrepancy Sampler that Distributes Monte Carlo Errors as a Blue Noise in Screen Space (Eric Heitz et al. (with Belcour, Ostromoukhov, Coeurjolly, Iehl), ACM SIGGRAPH 2019 Talks (follow-up: Belcour and Heitz, Lessons Learned and Improvements when Building Screen-Space Samplers with Blue-Noise Error Distribution, SIGGRAPH 2021 Talks, arXiv 2105.12620) 2019). Each pixel gets an Owen-scrambled Sobol sequence. Precomputed scrambling and ranking keys over a 128x128 tile are optimized offline so that the error of neighbouring pixels forms blue noise at every power-of-two spp up to 256. Fit: Medium. Table lookups are deterministic, and the CPU oracle can read the same tables. The tables take a storage buffer, which is scarce with 8 per stage, or a texture binding. They cover only 8 dimensions and up to 256 spp, so the gain stops at the first bounce. The 2021 talk shows that the optimization depends on the integrand. In WebGL2 the tables become a texture, which is natural there. Grad: neutral. Cost: medium. https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02150657
- [sampling] ART-Owen Scrambling (Abdalla G. M. Ahmed et al. (with Matt Pharr, Peter Wonka), ACM TOG, SIGGRAPH Asia 2023). Owen scrambling is generated from a grammar over adaptive regular tiles, built on the Thue-Morse word. It gives random access to samples in fixed time, is GPU friendly, scales to a chosen memory budget, can be optimized, and is invertible. Invertibility allows screen-space (blue-noise) scrambling of a high-dimensional Sobol sampler. Fit: Medium to high. Integer table lookups with fixed time complexity, so the result is deterministic and reproducible on the CPU oracle. A table must be bound. A small budget can go in a uniform buffer and so avoid a storage-buffer slot. The method is newer and less battle-tested than the Burley hash and ZSobol. Port the reference code and add golden tests before trusting it. WebGL2: works with integer GLSL ES 3.00 and a uniform block or texture. Grad: neutral. Cost: medium. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11664
- [sampling] SZ Sequences: Binary-Constructed (0, 2^q)-Sequences (Abdalla G. M. Ahmed et al. (with Matt Pharr, Victor Ostromoukhov, Hui Huang), ACM TOG, SIGGRAPH Asia (arXiv 2505.20434) 2025). Base-2 generator matrices are built from 2x2 block symbols (alphabets S and Z). The result is (0,4)-sequences, with (0,2)-sequences nested inside and nesting up to (0,16). Every 4D group, as well as its 2D pairs, is fully multi-stratified. The matrices are a drop-in replacement for Sobol matrices. Fit: High. Same bitwise u32 evaluation as Sobol, so it is deterministic, needs no extra buffers and matches the CPU oracle. The padded-4D design of the Burley sampler maps directly onto 4D-stratified groups. WebGL2: integer only, same bit-reversal note. Grad: neutral to positive (lower variance in the fitted loss). Cost: small. https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/ahmed2025/
- [sampling] Quad-Optimized Low-Discrepancy Sequences (Victor Ostromoukhov et al. (with Bonneel, Coeurjolly, Iehl), ACM SIGGRAPH 2024 Conference Papers 2024). A base-3 Sobol-style construction searches over irreducible polynomials and generator matrices. The result is (1,4)- or (2,4)-sequences in every consecutive quadruplet of dimensions and (0,2)-sequences in every consecutive pair. Fit: Medium to low. Base-3 digit extraction is integer division by 3 in a loop, which is deterministic but slower than bitwise Sobol. It has no cheap hash-based Owen scrambling. Powers of 3 do not match the power-of-two caps or a Morton-order ZSobol. Implement SZ first: it targets the same 4D stratification in base 2. Keep this paper as a comparison baseline. WebGL2 and grad: neutral. Cost: medium. https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/david.coeurjolly/publication/quadquad-24/
- [sampling] Spatiotemporal Blue Noise Masks (Alan Wolfe et al. (with Morrical, Akenine-Moller, Ramamoorthi), EGSR 2022 (Symposium Track); follow-up: Donnelly, Wolfe et al., Filter-Adapted Spatio-Temporal Sampling for Real-Time Rendering, I3D 2024 2022). The void-and-cluster algorithm and blue-noise dithered sampling are changed so that a stack of masks is blue noise in each frame and also well distributed over time at each pixel. Temporal filtering then converges fast and stays stable. FAST (I3D 2024) optimizes the mask spectrum for the actual spatial and temporal filter that will run. Fit: High for the real-time tier, low for the converging offline path tracer. Fixed precomputed tables (NVIDIA SDK masks, MIT-style license) are deterministic and easy to load in the CPU oracle. A texture binding does not consume a storage-buffer slot. The method fits WebGL2 well because it is just a texture fetch in a fragment pass. Grad: not relevant, except as dithering for the final image. Cost: small. https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09629
- [sampling] Filter Importance Sampling (classic; as implemented by the FilterSampler of pbrt-v4) (Manfred Ernst et al. (with Stamminger, Greiner), IEEE Symposium on Interactive Ray Tracing (RT 2006) 2006). Do not splat each sample into all pixels under a wide reconstruction filter. Instead, warp the pixel-sample offset by the filter's inverse CDF, so that each sample lands only in its own pixel with weight f/pdf (constant for a positive filter). This gives any filter shape (Gaussian, Blackman-Harris, Mitchell) with a plain per-pixel average. Fit: High. It avoids splatting, which on WebGPU compute would need float atomics or a separate gather pass. Float atomics do not exist, and an emulated version breaks the bit-identical determinism promise. Each pixel is still written only by its own invocation. The warp is a few f32 ops (the f32-only rule holds), or a small table in a uniform buffer. It works unchanged in a WebGL2 fragment-pass execution model. Grad: the weights are constant per sample, so the method is transparent to grad over shading. Cost: small. https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Sampling_and_Reconstruction/Image_Reconstruction
- [lights] Importance Sampling of Many Lights with Adaptive Tree Splitting (Alejandro Conty Estevez et al. (with Christopher Kulla), HPG 2018 (Proc. ACM Comput. Graph. Interact. Tech. 1(2)) [classic, first step] 2018). Build a BVH over all emitters that stores energy, spatial bounds and an orientation cone per node, then pick one light per shading point by descending the tree with a single random number weighted by each child's predicted contribution. Fit: High. Single-path descent without splitting is a short loop with no atomics, no wavefront change and one storage buffer that replaces the light table, so the 8-buffer budget does not grow. Deterministic given the per-pixel RNG. Works in a WebGL2 fragment pass with the tree in a float texture. Light selection is discrete and stays outside the grad path. Adaptive splitting gives a variable shadow-ray count per pixel. Leave it as an option. Cost: medium. https://doi.org/10.1145/3233305
- [lights] BRDF Importance Sampling for Polygonal Lights (Christoph Peters, ACM TOG 40(4) (SIGGRAPH 2021) 2021). Sample a convex polygonal light (a triangle is the simplest case) proportional to projected solid angle for diffuse surfaces and to a linearly transformed cosine for specular ones, with a robust two-iteration inversion and a faster, more stable solid-angle sampler as a by-product. Fit: High. Per-sample closed-form math in the existing megakernel, no new buffers, no atomics, no restructuring. Uses sqrt, atan2 and a fixed two-step iteration, so it adds operations that the determinism log must track as vendor-variable. It ports to WebGL2 unchanged. Sampling is not differentiated, so grad is not affected. Cost: small. https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/polygonal_lights.php
- [lights] MIS Compensation: Optimizing Sampling Techniques in Multiple Importance Sampling (Ondrej Karlik et al. (with Jaroslav Krivanek), ACM TOG 38(6) (SIGGRAPH Asia 2019) [classic for env-map NEE] 2019). When a tabulated sampling density is combined with BSDF sampling through MIS, subtract a constant (for example the mean) from the table before normalizing, so the table spends samples only where BSDF sampling is weak. Fit: High. All the extra work is host preprocessing that can run in f64 before the f32 upload. The kernel side is a deterministic binary search.
